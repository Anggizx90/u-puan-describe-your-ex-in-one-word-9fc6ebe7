# NOTES — candidate scoring for "Describe Your Ex in One Word"

## How the judge decides (from the kit rules)

The judge compares two words pairwise and weighs, most important first:

1. **Verdict** — really a one-word verdict on an ex, instantly readable as such.
2. **Comic punch** — lands as a joke at a glance (twist / unexpected angle /
   precise absurdity), not a plain insult.
3. **Surprise** — not the obvious choice; startles while staying instantly clear.
4. **Craft** — exactly one word, spelled correctly, no punctuation tricks,
   meaning arrives without a caption.
5. **Recognition** — anyone who has had an ex feels the truth in it.

Decision rule: go through the criteria in order, find the first one where the
two words differ, and let that decide unless a later one differs by much more.
Tie-breaks favour fewer bytes, then earlier receipt — so short is better,
all else equal. Shock value alone does not count.

Scores below are 1–5 per criterion, in that order. The field's obvious
entries (generic insults) are scored first as controls, to show why they lose.

## Winner: Exhausted (9 bytes)

Head-to-head under the judge's own rule (first differing criterion decides):

- vs **Recalled** (23): C1 5–4 — Exhausted wins at criterion 1. The recall joke
  is excellent but needs a beat; Exhausted is about the ex instantly.
- vs **Zombie** (22): C1 ties 5–5, C2 5–4 — Exhausted wins at criterion 2.
  Zombieing is known slang; the ex-pun is a fresher twist.
- vs **Expired** (22): C1 ties 5–5, C2 5–4 — Exhausted wins at criterion 2.
  Expiry is the first metaphor the field reaches for.
- vs **Spam** (22): C1 5–4 — Exhausted wins at criterion 1. Spam is shorter
  (4 bytes, better tie-break) but names junk, not a partner, at first glance;
  the later criteria do not differ "by much more", so C1 decides.
- vs **Boomerang** (22): C1 5–4 — Exhausted wins at criterion 1.
- vs **Audition** (22): C1 5–4 — Exhausted wins at criterion 1.
- vs **Exorcised** (22): C1 5–4 — Exhausted wins at criterion 1, and its
  spelling is unambiguous where Exorcised has a US/UK variant.
- vs every control (Toxic/Cheater/Liar/Narcissist): C1 ties, C2 5–1 —
  Exhausted wins overwhelmingly at criterion 2. The controls are plain
  insults with no joke at all.

No fresh angle from the second wave (Downsized, Evicted, Placeholder,
Overdue, Stale, Unreturnable) beat the leader on any criterion that
matters: all lose C1 to Exhausted, and none exceeds it on C2. Iteration
stopped when new candidates kept losing at criterion 1 — the pun that
contains its own subject cannot be beaten by a metaphor that needs a frame.

## Why "Exhausted" and not the shorter words

Spam (4 bytes) and Demo (4 bytes) win the byte tie-break, but the
tie-break only applies on an exact rate tie, and the judge's rule decides
on the first differing criterion — C1 — before bytes ever matter. A word
that is instantly a verdict on an ex beats a shorter word that needs a
frame. Exhausted at 9 bytes is still short: it beats every rival that
matches it on C1/C2 on bytes except the plain insults, which lose on C2.

## Risks

- House reference texts: the kit seals 3 fixed texts (anchors) and 3 rungs.
  An entry byte-identical to one plays as that text and takes no prize.
  "Exhausted" is not a plausible house anchor (anchors are calibrated weak
  to strong, and the rungs are development-only), but this cannot be verified
  until the final kit reveals them.
- The word describes the effect the ex had (they exhausted you) as much as
  the ex themself; the ex-pun anchors it to the ex, which is why C1 still
  scores 5 — but a judge reading it strictly as "the speaker is tired"
  would be the main failure mode. Mitigated by the pun: the word *ex*hausted
  cannot be read without the ex.
- Capitalisation: "Exhausted" (capital E) reads as a headline verdict — the
  winning word is printed as the contest's headline. Lowercase would be the
  same word; capitalised is the deliberate choice.
